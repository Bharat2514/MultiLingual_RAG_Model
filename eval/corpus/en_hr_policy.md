BHARAT TECHNOLOGIES PRIVATE LIMITED
EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK 2024
Leave, Attendance and Reimbursement Policy

Document Reference: HR/POL/2024/07
Effective Date: 1 April 2024
Approved By: The Board of Directors
Review Cycle: Annual

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This handbook sets out the leave entitlements, attendance obligations and reimbursement procedures applicable to all confirmed employees of Bharat Technologies Private Limited. It applies to every office of the company, including the registered office at Indore and the development centres at Pune and Bengaluru. Contractors engaged through a staffing agency are governed by their agency agreement and are outside the scope of this handbook.

Where a state law grants an entitlement more generous than the one recorded here, the state law prevails. Nothing in this handbook creates a contractual right to continued employment.

2. PROBATION AND CONFIRMATION

A newly appointed employee serves a probation period of six months from the date of joining. The reporting manager submits a confirmation assessment in the last fortnight of the fifth month. Where performance is assessed as below expectation, probation may be extended once by a further three months, and the extension must be communicated in writing before the original probation period expires. An employee on probation accrues casual leave but is not eligible for earned leave.

3. LEAVE ENTITLEMENTS

3.1 Earned Leave

A confirmed employee accrues earned leave at the rate of one and a half days for every completed calendar month of service, giving eighteen days in a full leave year. The leave year runs from 1 April to 31 March. Earned leave must be applied for at least seven working days in advance, except where the leave is occasioned by an emergency admitted by the reporting manager.

An employee may carry forward a maximum of forty five days of accumulated earned leave into the following leave year. Any balance above forty five days lapses on 31 March and is not encashable. On separation, accumulated earned leave up to forty five days is encashed at the basic salary rate applicable on the last working day.

3.2 Casual Leave

Casual leave is granted at twelve days per leave year and is credited in full on 1 April. Casual leave cannot be carried forward, cannot be encashed, and cannot be combined with earned leave in a single application. No more than three days of casual leave may be taken consecutively.

3.3 Sick Leave

Sick leave is granted at twelve days per leave year. A medical certificate from a registered practitioner is required where sick leave exceeds two consecutive working days. Unused sick leave may be accumulated up to a ceiling of sixty days and may be used only for a genuine illness; it is never encashable.

3.4 Maternity and Paternity Leave

A female employee is entitled to twenty six weeks of paid maternity leave for the first two surviving children and twelve weeks thereafter, in line with the Maternity Benefit Act. A male employee is entitled to fifteen days of paid paternity leave, which must be availed within six months of the date of birth.

4. ATTENDANCE

4.1 Working Hours

The standard working week is forty hours spread over five days, Monday to Friday. Core hours during which every employee must be reachable are 11:00 to 16:00 India Standard Time. Attendance is captured by the access control system at each office and, for remote work, by the sign-in record in the company intranet.

4.2 Remote Work

An employee may work remotely for up to eight days in a calendar month with the prior approval of the reporting manager. A request for a continuous remote period exceeding ten working days requires the additional approval of the functional head. The company does not reimburse home internet or electricity charges for routine remote work.

4.3 Late Marks and Absence

Three late marks in a calendar month are treated as one day of casual leave. An absence of three consecutive working days without intimation is treated as unauthorised absence and attracts a written warning. Unauthorised absence exceeding eight consecutive working days is treated as voluntary abandonment of service, and the matter is referred to the Head of Human Resources.

5. HOLIDAYS

The company observes ten public holidays in a calendar year. Three of these are fixed nationally, being Republic Day, Independence Day and Gandhi Jayanti. The remaining seven are declared by each office in December for the following year, so that regional festivals are properly represented. Employees may additionally take two restricted holidays of their choice from the published list.

6. TRAVEL AND REIMBURSEMENT

6.1 Domestic Travel

Domestic air travel is permitted in economy class only. An employee at grade M3 and above may travel by air for a journey exceeding five hundred kilometres; below grade M3, air travel requires the prior written approval of the functional head. Rail travel is reimbursed at AC 2-tier for grade M2 and above and AC 3-tier below that grade.

The daily allowance for domestic travel is eighteen hundred rupees per day in a metropolitan city and twelve hundred rupees per day elsewhere. The daily allowance covers meals and incidentals and does not require the submission of individual bills.

6.2 Accommodation

Hotel accommodation is reimbursed against an original tax invoice, subject to a ceiling of six thousand rupees per night in a metropolitan city and four thousand rupees per night elsewhere. Where an employee stays with family or friends, a lump sum of one thousand rupees per night is payable in lieu of a hotel bill.

6.3 Claim Submission

A reimbursement claim must be submitted through the expense portal within thirty days of the completion of travel. A claim submitted after thirty days but within sixty days requires a written justification and the approval of the functional head. A claim submitted after sixty days is not payable under any circumstance.

Claims approved before the twentieth of a month are settled with that month's payroll. Claims approved after the twentieth are settled in the following payroll cycle.

7. LEARNING AND DEVELOPMENT

Every employee is entitled to a professional development allowance of twenty five thousand rupees per financial year, which may be applied to certifications, conference fees or online courses relevant to the employee's role. The allowance does not carry forward. Where the company sponsors a certification costing more than fifty thousand rupees, the employee signs a service commitment of twelve months from the date of certification.

8. GRIEVANCE AND DISCIPLINE

An employee who wishes to raise a grievance about the application of this policy should first approach the reporting manager. If the matter is unresolved after ten working days, it may be escalated in writing to the Head of Human Resources, who responds within fifteen working days. A grievance concerning the reporting manager may be raised directly with the Head of Human Resources without the first step.

Disciplinary action follows a graded sequence: verbal counselling, written warning, final written warning and termination. A show cause notice is issued before any final written warning, and the employee is given seven days to respond.

9. AMENDMENT

This handbook may be amended by the Board of Directors. An amendment takes effect thirty days after it is published on the company intranet, except where an earlier date is required by law.
