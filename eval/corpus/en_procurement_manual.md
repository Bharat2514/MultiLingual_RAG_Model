INDORE MUNICIPAL CORPORATION
GENERAL FINANCIAL AND PROCUREMENT MANUAL

Chapter 4 — Purchase of Goods and Services
Issued under the standing orders of the Commissioner

1. SCOPE OF THE CHAPTER

This chapter prescribes the procedure for the purchase of goods and the procurement of services other than works contracts, which are dealt with in Chapter 6. It applies to every department and zonal office of the Corporation.

Purchases are to be made in the most economical manner consistent with the quality required, and the officer authorising a purchase is personally responsible for satisfying himself that the price is reasonable.

2. METHODS OF PROCUREMENT

2.1 Direct Purchase

Goods of a value up to twenty five thousand rupees on each occasion may be purchased directly without inviting quotations, on a certificate recorded by the purchasing officer that the goods are of the requisite quality and the price is reasonable.

2.2 Purchase by Quotation

Where the value exceeds twenty five thousand rupees but does not exceed two lakh fifty thousand rupees, at least three quotations shall be obtained from registered suppliers. The quotations shall be opened by a committee of not fewer than three officers.

2.3 Limited Tender

Where the value exceeds two lakh fifty thousand rupees but does not exceed twenty five lakh rupees, a limited tender shall be issued to suppliers on the approved panel, and the notice period shall not be less than ten days.

2.4 Open Tender

Where the value exceeds twenty five lakh rupees, an open tender shall be published on the e-procurement portal with a notice period of not less than twenty one days. The notice period may be reduced to fourteen days in a case of urgency recorded in writing by the Commissioner.

3. RATE CONTRACTS

The Corporation may enter into a rate contract for items of recurring consumption. A rate contract remains in force for two years and may be extended by one year where the rates remain advantageous. A department purchasing an item covered by a live rate contract shall not invite fresh quotations for it.

Where the Government e-Marketplace carries the item, procurement through that platform is preferred, and a departure requires the reasons to be recorded.

4. FINANCIAL POWERS

The financial powers for sanctioning a purchase are as follows. A Zonal Officer may sanction up to two lakh rupees. A Head of Department may sanction up to ten lakh rupees. The Additional Commissioner may sanction up to fifty lakh rupees. The Commissioner may sanction up to two crore rupees. Above two crore rupees, the sanction of the Mayor in Council is required.

A purchase shall not be split into parts so as to bring it within a lower sanctioning power. Splitting is treated as a financial irregularity and is reported to the audit committee.

5. SUPPLIER REGISTRATION

A supplier seeking registration files an application with the goods and services tax registration certificate, the permanent account number, the last two years of audited accounts and a solvency certificate from a scheduled bank. Registration is valid for three years and is renewable on application made not later than sixty days before expiry.

A supplier who fails to execute an accepted order twice within a registration period is removed from the panel and may not reapply for two years.

6. INSPECTION AND ACCEPTANCE

Goods received are inspected within seven days of delivery by an officer other than the one who placed the order. The inspection report records the quantity, the conformity to specification and any shortage or damage. Payment is not released against an uninspected delivery.

Where goods are rejected, the supplier is informed within three days and shall remove them within fifteen days, failing which the Corporation may dispose of them at the supplier's risk and cost.

7. PAYMENT

A bill for supplies is passed within fifteen working days of the inspection report, and payment is released within thirty days of the bill being passed. Interest is not payable on delayed payment for supplies except where the contract expressly provides for it.

Advance payment is not ordinarily permitted. Where an advance is unavoidable it shall not exceed thirty percent of the order value and shall be secured by a bank guarantee of equal amount.

8. DISPOSAL OF SURPLUS STORES

Stores declared surplus or unserviceable by a survey committee are disposed of by public auction or by e-auction. The reserve price is fixed by the survey committee, and a sale below the reserve price requires the Commissioner's approval. Sale proceeds are credited to the Corporation's general revenue.

9. AUDIT AND RECORD KEEPING

Every purchase file is retained for eight years from the date of final payment, or until the completion of audit, whichever is later. The internal audit wing examines a sample of not less than ten percent of purchases each quarter, and reports irregularities to the Commissioner and the audit committee.
