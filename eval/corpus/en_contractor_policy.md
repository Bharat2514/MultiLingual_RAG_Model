BHARAT TECHNOLOGIES PRIVATE LIMITED
CONTRACTOR AND CONSULTANT ENGAGEMENT POLICY

Document Reference: PROC/CON/2024/09
Effective Date: 1 May 2024
Owner: Head of Procurement

1. PURPOSE

This policy governs the engagement of individual contractors, consultants and staffing agencies who provide services to the company but are not on its employee rolls. It exists because the obligations that attach to an engagement of this kind — tax withholding, intellectual property assignment, statutory contribution and access control — differ materially from those that attach to employment, and treating the two alike has repeatedly produced disputes.

Nothing in this policy makes a contractor an employee, and no clause here should be read as extending the employee handbook to a contractor.

2. CATEGORIES OF ENGAGEMENT

A retained consultant provides advisory work against a defined statement of work and a fixed fee. A time and materials contractor is billed against hours worked at an agreed rate. An agency resource is engaged through a staffing partner, who remains the employer of record. A fixed capacity arrangement reserves a named team from a partner firm for an agreed duration.

Only the first two categories may be engaged directly by a business unit. The other two require the Head of Procurement to countersign.

3. ENGAGEMENT PROCESS

3.1 Justification

A business unit seeking to engage a contractor raises a request stating the deliverable, the duration, the estimated cost and why the work cannot be done by existing staff. A request that describes an ongoing role rather than a bounded deliverable is returned; ongoing roles are filled through recruitment.

3.2 Approval Thresholds

An engagement with a total value up to five lakh rupees is approved by the functional head. Above five lakh rupees and up to twenty five lakh rupees, the approval of the Chief Financial Officer is additionally required. Above twenty five lakh rupees, the engagement requires Board approval and a competitive selection from at least three quotations.

3.3 Review Period

A newly engaged contractor works an initial review period of three months. At the end of that period the engaging manager records whether the deliverables were met. Where they were not, the engagement is terminated with fifteen days written notice and no further liability arises.

3.4 Duration Limits

A direct contractor engagement may not exceed twenty four months in aggregate, including renewals. Beyond that, the arrangement must either end or be converted to employment. This limit exists to keep long-running engagements from being used as a substitute for headcount, which creates exposure under labour law.

4. COMMERCIAL TERMS

4.1 Invoicing

Invoices are raised monthly in arrears, supported by an approved timesheet where the engagement is on a time and materials basis. An invoice received by the fifth of a month is processed in that month's payment run; later invoices move to the following month.

4.2 Payment Terms

Payment terms are thirty days from the receipt of a valid invoice. An invoice that lacks the purchase order number, the goods and services tax registration number or the approved timesheet is not a valid invoice and the thirty day clock does not begin until the defect is cured.

4.3 Withholding

Tax is deducted at source at the rate applicable to the category of service. A contractor who has not furnished a permanent account number is subject to deduction at the higher rate prescribed by the Income Tax Act. Deduction certificates are issued quarterly.

4.4 Expenses

Out of pocket expenses are reimbursed only where the statement of work says so expressly, and then only against original receipts. In the absence of an express clause, the fee is deemed inclusive of all expenses.

5. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AND CONFIDENTIALITY

All work product created in the course of an engagement vests in the company from the moment of creation, and the contractor assigns any residual right by the engagement letter. A contractor may not reuse code, designs or documents produced for the company in any other engagement.

A contractor signs a confidentiality undertaking before any access is granted. The undertaking survives the engagement by five years. Where the engagement touches customer personal data, the contractor additionally signs the data processing addendum and completes the company's data protection training.

6. ACCESS AND OFFBOARDING

A contractor receives a separate account namespace so that contractor access can be enumerated and revoked as a set. Contractor accounts carry a hard expiry date equal to the engagement end date, and an extension requires the engagement to be extended first — an account cannot outlive its engagement.

On the last day of an engagement the engaging manager confirms the return of company devices and the removal of company data from any contractor-owned device. Access is revoked automatically at the account expiry date whether or not that confirmation has been recorded.

7. PROHIBITED ARRANGEMENTS

The following are not permitted: engaging a former employee as a contractor within six months of separation without the Head of Human Resources' approval; engaging a relative of an employee involved in the selection without a recorded conflict declaration; paying a contractor in cash; and engaging any party appearing on a sanctions list.

8. RECORDS

Procurement maintains a register of every live engagement with its value, duration, approver and expiry. The register is reviewed by the Chief Financial Officer each quarter, and engagements approaching the twenty four month limit are flagged three months in advance.
