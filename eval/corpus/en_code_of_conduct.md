BHARAT TECHNOLOGIES PRIVATE LIMITED
CODE OF CONDUCT AND PREVENTION OF WORKPLACE HARASSMENT

Document Reference: HR/COC/2024/01
Effective Date: 1 April 2024
Approved By: The Board of Directors

1. APPLICATION

This code applies to every employee, intern and contractor of the company, at every location, and at any event that is an extension of the workplace — a client meeting, an offsite, a conference and a work-related messaging group all count. It applies regardless of seniority; the code is enforced more strictly, not less, against those with authority over others.

2. CORE OBLIGATIONS

Deal honestly with colleagues, customers and regulators. Record transactions accurately. Do not offer or accept anything of value in exchange for a business decision. Protect information entrusted to you. Speak up about conduct that appears to breach this code, and do not retaliate against anyone who does.

3. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

A conflict of interest arises where your personal interest could reasonably be seen to influence a decision you take for the company. Common cases are: a financial interest in a supplier, a customer or a competitor; a family member employed by any of them; outside employment; and participation in a selection decision involving a relative or close friend.

A conflict must be declared in writing to your functional head as soon as it arises, and in any event before the decision it touches is taken. A declared conflict is usually managed by recusal, not by prohibition — what is prohibited is the failure to declare.

Every employee at grade M2 and above files an annual conflict declaration in April, whether or not a conflict exists.

4. GIFTS AND HOSPITALITY

A gift of a value up to two thousand rupees may be accepted without approval. Above that value, a gift must be declined, or where declining would cause offence, accepted on behalf of the company and handed to Human Resources. Cash, gift cards and anything convertible to cash may never be accepted at any value.

Ordinary business hospitality — a meal at a business meeting, standard conference attendance — is permitted. Travel, accommodation or entertainment paid for by a supplier requires the prior written approval of the functional head.

No gift or hospitality of any value may be offered to or accepted from a public official.

5. PREVENTION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT

The company maintains an Internal Committee constituted under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act. The Committee has a presiding officer who is a woman employed at a senior level, two members from among employees committed to the cause of women, and one external member from a non-governmental organisation familiar with the issues.

A complaint may be made in writing to the Internal Committee within three months of the incident, and the Committee may extend that period by a further three months where it is satisfied that circumstances prevented an earlier complaint.

The Committee completes its inquiry within ninety days and submits its report within ten days of completing the inquiry. The employer acts on the recommendations within sixty days of receiving the report.

Conciliation may be attempted at the request of the complainant, but no monetary settlement may be made the basis of conciliation, and conciliation is never proposed by the Committee itself.

6. CONFIDENTIALITY OF COMPLAINTS

The identity of the complainant, the respondent, the witnesses, the inquiry proceedings and the recommendations are confidential. Publishing any of it attracts a penalty under the Act and is separately treated as misconduct under this code.

7. RETALIATION

Retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good faith, or who participates in an inquiry, is itself a serious breach of this code and is dealt with as misconduct independently of the outcome of the original complaint. A good faith complaint that is not upheld is not a false complaint, and no action follows from it.

A complaint that the Committee finds to have been made maliciously and with knowledge of its falsity may be acted upon, but the standard of proof for that finding is the same as for the original allegation.

8. RAISING A CONCERN

Concerns may be raised with the line manager, with the Head of Human Resources, or through the confidential ethics line, which is operated by an external provider and permits anonymous reporting. Every report is acknowledged within three working days and its outcome recorded.

9. CONSEQUENCES

A breach of this code is misconduct and is dealt with under the disciplinary procedure. Consequences range from counselling to termination, and where the conduct is also an offence, the company reports it to the appropriate authority. Reimbursement of any improper gain is required in every case.

10. ANNUAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT

Every employee acknowledges this code on joining and each April thereafter. Failure to acknowledge does not excuse a breach.
